### **13.1 Expanded Prioritization Framework/Rubric**

#### **Criteria and Definitions:**

1. **Business Value:**  
   - **Definition:** Measures the potential return on investment (ROI) and alignment with organizational objectives. This includes revenue impact, cost savings, risk mitigation, and customer satisfaction.  
   - **Example Indicators:** Increased revenue potential, reduced operational costs, improved customer experience, enhanced market competitiveness.

2. **Technical Feasibility:**  
   - **Definition:** Evaluates the practicality of implementing the project with current technology, tools, and expertise.  
   - **Example Indicators:** Technology maturity, complexity of integration, dependencies on legacy systems, level of technical debt.

3. **Resource Availability:**  
   - **Definition:** Assesses whether sufficient personnel, budget, and tools are available to execute the project successfully.  
   - **Example Indicators:** Availability of skilled engineers, time commitment required, access to required tools, budget constraints.

4. **Alignment with Strategic Goals:**  
   - **Definition:** Ensures the project aligns with the company’s broader strategic objectives, including long-term vision and priorities.  
   - **Example Indicators:** Alignment with quarterly/annual OKRs, contribution to key strategic initiatives, executive sponsorship.

---

#### **Scoring System:**  
Each criterion is scored on a **1-5 scale**, with standardized definitions for each score:

- **1:** Very Low – Minimal impact or feasibility; significant blockers.  
- **2:** Low – Limited impact; moderate blockers.  
- **3:** Neutral – Balanced pros and cons; average impact.  
- **4:** High – Clear positive impact; minor blockers.  
- **5:** Very High – Significant strategic importance; minimal blockers.

#### **Weighted Scoring:**  
Some criteria may be given higher weight based on organizational focus. Example weighting:  
- Business Value: 35%  
- Technical Feasibility: 25%  
- Resource Availability: 20%  
- Strategic Alignment: 20%

| **Criteria**          | **Score (1-5)** | **Weight (%)** | **Weighted Score** |
|------------------------|---------------|---------------|--------------------|
| Business Value        | 4             | 35            | 1.4               |

| **Total Weighted Score** |               | **100%**     | **3.95**          |

**Decision Threshold:** Projects scoring above **3.5** are considered high-priority.

---

#### **Team Involvement:**  
- **Who Participates:** Core Council Members (e.g., Council Chair, SRE Representatives, Product Managers, Technical Architects).  
- **Typical Size:** 6–10 members.  
- **Roles:**  
   - **Facilitator:** Ensures discussion remains focused.  
   - **Timekeeper:** Keeps the meeting on track.  
   - **Documenter:** Captures scores and decisions.

---

#### **Voting Process:**
1. **Individual Scoring Round:** Each participant assigns scores to each criterion independently.  
2. **Discussion Round:** Members share reasoning behind their scores.  
3. **Final Consensus Vote:** Members may adjust their scores after discussion.  
4. **Scoring Method:** Final scores are aggregated, and the weighted score is calculated.  

**Voting Mechanism:**  
- Default: Open voting with transparent discussion.  
- Alternative (if high sensitivity/conflict arises): Anonymous scoring using a digital tool or survey.

---

#### **Best Practices from Leading Tech Companies:**
- **Google:** Uses weighted prioritization matrices with a focus on strategic alignment and technical feasibility.  
- **Microsoft:** Emphasizes cross-team alignment and transparent scoring processes.  
- **Amazon:** Focuses heavily on business value and resource efficiency, often using "Working Backwards" to prioritize customer outcomes.  
- **Spotify:** Employs squad-level input to ensure practical resource alignment before decisions are made.
